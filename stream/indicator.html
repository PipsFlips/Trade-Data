Chart legend / how the system is coded
0–10 weighted evidence score
Scores are displayed on a 0–10 scale. A score such as 8.5/10 means the system's weighted inputs are strongly aligned with that move, setup, or structure. It is not a probability of success. Internal component weights are normalized to the 0–10 display scale.
1m Delta, 5m Delta, Raw Session CVD, Raw NY CVD
Delta is aggressive buy volume minus aggressive sell volume for the bar. Positive means more contracts traded at the offer; negative means more traded at the bid. 1m Delta and 5m Delta are the current local order-flow buckets. Raw Session CVD is cumulative API trade-side delta for the CME session beginning at 15:00 PT. Raw NY CVD is the same calculation for RTH only, 06:30–13:00 PT, and is hidden outside that window.
PDH / PDL / PDC / PWH / PWL
PDH = previous regular-session high. PDL = previous regular-session low. PDC = previous regular-session close. PWH/PWL = previous CME trading-week high/low. These are treated as major reference/liquidity levels and can become breakout, rejection, trap, or target locations when price is close enough to them.
ON High / Low
ON means the overnight window from 15:00 PT to 06:30 PT. ON High/Low are the highest and lowest traded prices in that window and freeze at the RTH open. Overnight VAH/VAL/POC are kept out of the visible Profiles layer; use the Sessions layer for the overnight extremes.
Asia High / Low and London High / Low
Asia is 20:00–03:00 ET. London is 03:00–08:30 ET. These America/New_York windows automatically follow daylight saving time; each end time is excluded from its window. The high and low of each window become reference/liquidity levels. They are useful for sweeps, rejections, breakouts, and targets because price often reacts around prior session extremes.
Prev VAH / POC / VAL
These are the previous RTH volume-profile levels. VAH/VAL enclose the 70% value area; POC is the highest-volume price. The live engine uses profile location as context: price above value adds bullish context, below value adds bearish context, and the POC is treated as a major reference.
Session VWAP and NY VWAP
Session VWAP resets at 15:00 PT and runs through the CME session. NY VWAP resets at 06:30 PT and is active only through 13:00 PT. Historical chart curves use 5-minute typical price × volume; the current live value can use exact received trades when the relay is healthy. Price above the active VWAP contributes bullish context; below contributes bearish context.
4H pivot levels
A 4-hour pivot is a local swing high or swing low built from completed CME-session-aligned 4H candles. The 4H windows are 18:00–22:00, 22:00–02:00, 02:00–06:00, 06:00–10:00, 10:00–14:00 ET, plus the shortened 14:00–17:00 ET pre-maintenance candle. A pivot requires two completed 4H candles on each side for confirmation. Untouched pivots receive more weight; significance also considers later excursion, distance from current price, and age.
Iceberg / Absorption — exact price vs 3–5m zone
ICEBERG remains the narrow exact-price detector: aggressive trades must repeatedly hit one price while displayed depth replenishes. ABSORPTION is a separate rolling 3–5 minute zone detector. It looks for one-sided aggressive delta that produces less price progress than expected, rejection from the local extreme, elevated volume, repeated bid/ask replenishment across nearby prices, and major-level confluence. A BUY ABSORPTION zone means aggressive sellers are being absorbed by passive buyers; SELL ABSORPTION is the reverse. The 0–10 score is evidence alignment, not reversal probability, and absorption by itself is not a trade trigger.
Close Pressure — final 30 minutes
Visible only from 12:30–13:00 PT. This is a collector-derived closing-pressure proxy, not exchange MOC imbalance data. The 0–10 score weights recent 1-minute delta, delta acceleration, price displacement versus ATR, NY VWAP location, nearby visible bid/ask liquidity, and inferred iceberg/absorption activity. BUY/SELL indicates directional pressure; BALANCED means the evidence is not sufficiently one-sided.
Market Structure — Trend / Range / Transition
Market Structure uses completed 5-minute price action with 15-minute confirmation. The trend score weights directional efficiency, normalized slope, HH/HL or LH/LL structure, rolling VWAP alignment, and 15-minute agreement. The range score weights low directional efficiency, candle overlap, VWAP crossings, compressed range width relative to ATR, and neutral swing structure. RANGE is drawn as a horizontal shaded box. TREND UP/DOWN is drawn as an adaptive regression channel: the centerline is the recent 5-minute regression fit, while the parallel upper/lower bands use 1.75× residual standard deviation with an ATR-based minimum width. TRANSITION means neither side has enough aligned evidence yet.
Edgeful historical context
Edgeful is used as a historical-condition layer, not as live order flow and not as a replacement for the collector. The panel can surface overnight-range behavior, 15-minute ORB behavior, 60-minute Initial Balance behavior, prior-day high/low follow-through, RTH gap-fill frequency, opening-candle continuation, first-break extension and retracement behavior, and ATR/ADR context when the corresponding live condition exists. The live analysis uses matching Edgeful statistics as low-weight directional checks for bias and buy/sell suggestions; current collector price, structure, delta, VWAP, and liquidity remain the primary evidence. London-to-New-York direction correlation is currently excluded because the trailing sample was approximately 50/50 and did not show a useful directional edge.
11:00–12:00 PT Midday ORB
The system builds a separate range from 11:00–12:00 PT, freezes its high/low at noon, and watches the final hour into the 13:00 PT close. The current Edgeful trailing-12-month one-side, double-break, no-break, continuation-extension, and retracement frequencies are loaded from the weekly Edgeful snapshot and shown in the live context/setup text. The Edgeful chart button opens selectable conditions; a purple range or level shows the selected collector reference. These are historical frequencies, not trade probabilities. The live engine only promotes a setup after a 5-minute close outside the range and still requires current delta/VWAP/bias confirmation.
ORB — Opening Range Breakout
ORB High/Low are built from the first 15 minutes of RTH, 06:30–06:45 PT. A breakout setup is only considered when price is near the appropriate ORB boundary, currently within roughly min(25 pts, max(10 pts, 0.75 × ATR5)). The grade starts at 25 and adds: bias alignment +20, volume ≥1.2× recent 5m median +15, aligned closed 5m delta +15, VWAP alignment +10, next target available +10, and aligned strong trap signal +5; capped at 100.
15m OB — Order Block
OBs are detected on aggregated 15-minute bars. The origin candle must be opposite the subsequent displacement direction. The next 1–3 bars must break the recent 6-bar swing by at least two ticks and travel at least 1.25× 15m ATR. A bullish OB uses the origin candle's open/low zone; a bearish OB uses open/high. It is discarded if later closes invalidate the far side. Freshness is based on retests: ≤1 touch is considered fresh. A setup is shown only when the OB scores at least 65 and price is within min(20 pts, max(8 pts, 0.9 × ATR5)).
OB grading
An OB begins at 25 points. It receives +15 for displacement ≥1.25× ATR, +10 for strong first impulse-body quality, +10 if fresh, +10 for nearby major-level confluence, +10 for VWAP alignment, +10 for aligned order-flow delta, and +10 when there is at least ~1.5R room to the next major target. Maximum = 100.
Trap / confirmed trap
A buyer trap occurs when price sweeps above a major level by at least two ticks with positive 1m delta, closes back below the level, and the next closed minute fails to reclaim it. That implies trapped aggressive buyers and produces a SELL-side reversal context. A seller trap is the inverse: sweep below support with negative delta, reclaim above, then follow-through holds; that produces BUY-side context. Traps expire from the active signal after about 20 minutes and are invalidated by later acceptance through the swept level.
Trap grading
The legacy trap score starts with confirmed trap +25, then can add: closed 5m structure +20, aligned closed 1m delta +10, aligned closed 5m delta +10, session CVD aligned for at least 10 seconds +10, VWAP alignment +10, major-level confluence +10, and adequate target room +5. The active top-right score is no longer trap-only; this trap score is one candidate input to the broader setup engine.
Iceberg / absorption
This is a probable hidden-liquidity detector, not direct visibility of hidden orders. It combines executed trades with Level-2 depth replenishment at the same price. Repeated aggressive buys plus ask replenishment suggest a passive seller / possible sell iceberg; repeated aggressive sells plus bid replenishment suggest a passive buyer / possible buy iceberg. Strong recent detections can add ±2 to market bias and can generate a conditional setup after rejection/reclaim confirmation. Depth-side mapping should still be treated as inferred until fully validated against live payload behavior.
Open gaps
Two gaps are tracked. Session gap: the prior pre-maintenance close versus the 15:00 PT CME reopen. RTH gap: the prior RTH close versus the next 06:30 PT open. A gap disappears once filled. A gap-fill setup is considered only when price is near an unfilled gap; proximity is currently max(12 pts, 0.65 × ATR5).
Gap grading
A gap-fill setup starts at 30. It adds +20 for directional bias alignment, +20 when both closed 1m and 5m delta align, +10 for VWAP alignment, +10 for relative 5m volume ≥1.15× recent median, and +10 for an aligned strong trap signal. Maximum = 100.
Live market bias
Bias is a separate context score, not a trade probability. Current weights: active VWAP ±2, profile location ±1, closed 5m structure ±1, closed 5m delta ±1, Session CVD ±1, 15m delta trend ±2, recent confirmed trap ±2, recent strong iceberg ±2, and a strong aligned trap signal ±2. Bullish begins at +3, Bearish at −3, Neutral is −1 to +1, and the in-between states are Mixed. The displayed analysis must persist about 15 seconds before replacing the prior state.
Top-right BUY / SELL / NEUTRAL grade
This is the best current actionable setup grade, not a probability of success. The server ranks qualified setups and uses the highest numeric quality score. A candidate must score at least 50 and remain the same directional/setup candidate for about 12 seconds before becoming active. If the setup disappears, the prior signal is held for about 15 seconds before clearing. This is designed to prevent tick-by-tick flipping.
Major-level breakout / breakdown grading
Generic breakout/breakdown setups are only considered when price is close to the nearest major level, currently within min(30 pts, max(12 pts, ATR5)). Base grade = 45. It adds +15 for aligned closed 5m delta, +10 for VWAP alignment, and +15 for aligned 15m delta trend. Maximum = 85 under the current formula.
MNQ / MES market toggle
The top toggle switches the entire dashboard between MNQ and MES. Each market has its own contract, realtime relay state, delta/CVD, VWAP, profile, levels, order blocks, traps, gaps, and setup grades. The scoring model is structurally the same, but MES uses tighter point-distance caps so proximity logic scales appropriately to the smaller index range.
Chart time ranges: 1h–8h / 1D / 1W / 1M
The short views (1h–8h) use live 5-minute candles. 1D uses the most recent 24 hours of 5-minute candles. 1W uses hourly candles for the most recent week. 1M uses daily candles for roughly the last 30 trading/calendar days available. Horizontal market levels remain available on all views; intraday-only overlays such as session VWAP curves, session-start markers, and the 5m delta histogram are suppressed on the higher-timeframe views to keep them readable.
ATR5 and proximity filters
ATR5 is the recent average true range of 5-minute bars and is used to scale distance filters to current volatility. Proximity filters prevent stale recommendations from remaining active when price is far from the relevant level. OB, ORB, major-level, and gap setups each use separate capped ATR-based distance rules.
Resting liquidity / standing DOM orders
Resting liquidity is visible limit-order size currently standing in the order book. Large BID size is potential passive buying support; large ASK size is potential passive selling resistance. It is not treated as a buy/sell signal by itself because orders can be cancelled or moved. The detector compares nearby visible depth against the current book's median size and flags unusually large levels (dynamic threshold, with separate minimum floors for MNQ and MES). Holding means the size remains posted, Being Hit means recent trades are interacting with the level while displayed size is decreasing, and Replenishing means displayed size increases again after interaction. Pulled means a large order disappeared without nearby execution evidence; Consumed means it disappeared while trades were occurring at that price. Only the strongest nearby current levels are marked on the chart. Visible DOM liquidity can be spoofed/pulled, so confirmation from executions, absorption, delta/CVD, and price response is more important than displayed size alone.
Colors and chart toggles
Green generally marks support/buy-side references; red marks resistance/sell-side references; Asia levels are yellow; London levels are blue; weekly levels are white; previous POC is dark red. The chart buttons can independently hide/show Sessions, Prior H/L, Profiles, 4H pivots, 15m OB, ORB, VWAP, and Traps. Hidden session layers are also removed from chart autoscaling so they do not compress the candles.
